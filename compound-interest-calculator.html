About the Compound Interest Calculator

Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest. See how a starting amount and regular deposits can grow over time.

How to use it

  1. Enter your starting amount and any monthly deposit.
  2. Add the yearly interest rate and the number of years.
  3. Pick how often interest is compounded and read the final balance.

The formula

Balance after each month = previous balance × (1 + monthly rate) + deposit

The monthly rate comes from the yearly rate and the compounding frequency you choose.

Worked examples

Start with 5,000, add 200 a month at 7% for 20 years. The final balance is $124,379.03. You put in $53,000.00, and $71,379.03 is interest.

₹1 lakh plus ₹5,000 a month at 8% for 10 years grows to ₹11,36,694.20, with ₹4,36,694.20 earned as interest.

Common mistakes to avoid

Beginners often use the simple rate and forget that the deposit timing and compounding frequency matter. Another mistake is ignoring inflation and taxes, which make real growth lower than the figure shown. Also avoid assuming a high return for decades, since long-run returns are usually lower than recent good years.

Tips

  • Time matters more than the rate. Starting early beats starting big.
  • Monthly deposits are the biggest lever for most people. Automate them.
  • More frequent compounding helps, but the difference between monthly and daily is small.
  • Remember that inflation reduces what the final balance can buy.

Frequently asked questions

What is compound interest?

It is interest calculated on both your original money and the interest already added. That is why growth speeds up over time.

Does compounding frequency matter?

Slightly. Monthly beats yearly at the same rate because interest is added to the balance sooner. The effective annual rate row shows the real yearly rate.

Are the deposits made at the start or end of the month?

The calculator adds each deposit at the end of the month, which is a cautious assumption.

Is the result guaranteed?

No. Real investments rise and fall. Use the calculator to compare scenarios, not to predict returns.

Worked example: 10,000 start with 500 a month

Suppose you start with SAR 10,000, add SAR 500 every month, and earn 6% a year, compounded monthly for 10 years.

  1. Monthly rate: 6% ÷ 12 = 0.5% per month.
  2. Number of months: 10 × 12 = 120.
  3. Final balance: about SAR 100,134.
  4. Total you put in: 10,000 + 500 × 120 = SAR 70,000.
  5. Interest earned: about SAR 30,134.

Notice that the interest earned is more than 40% of your total deposits. The longer the time, the more the growth builds on itself.

Why monthly compounding matters

A 6% yearly rate compounded monthly gives an effective annual rate of about 6.17%. The difference looks small, but over 20 or 30 years it adds up. Compare the frequency options in the calculator to see how much it changes your result.

How regular deposits help

Starting early matters more than the amount at the start. Someone who saves SAR 300 a month from age 25 can often end up with more at 55 than someone who starts at 40 with SAR 600 a month, if the rate stays similar. Try changing the starting year in the calculator to see this effect.

Limits of the estimate

The calculator assumes one fixed rate for the whole period. Real savings accounts, mutual funds and shares can rise and fall. Fees, taxes and inflation also reduce the real value of your money. Treat the result as a planning guide and check the actual rules of your bank or fund.

More questions about compound growth

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original amount. Compound interest is calculated on the original amount plus the interest already earned, so the balance grows faster over time.

Does a higher compounding frequency always help?

It helps a little, because interest is added more often. The gain gets smaller as the frequency rises, and the annual rate matters much more than the frequency.

Can I use this for a loan or debt?

The same logic applies, but the balance grows against you. For loans, use the Loan Calculator, which is built for monthly repayments.

Understand compounding frequency

Compound interest earns returns on both the original amount and previously credited interest. The result depends on the rate, time period, contribution schedule and how often interest is compounded. A nominal annual rate compounded monthly is not identical to the same nominal rate compounded annually.

Using the Compound Interest Calculator wisely

For a fair comparison, use the same deposit amount, term, compounding frequency and fee assumptions for each offer. Actual savings products may use daily balances, variable rates, taxes or withdrawal penalties that are not represented by a simplified estimate.
