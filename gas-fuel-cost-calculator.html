About the Fuel Cost Calculator

Work out what a trip will cost in fuel. Use miles or kilometers, MPG or km/L, and litres or gallons, in your own currency.

How to use it

  1. Enter the trip distance and pick miles or kilometers.
  2. Enter your fuel economy and its unit, or tap a vehicle type for a typical figure.
  3. Enter the fuel price per litre or gallon, tick round trip if needed, and read the cost.

The formula

Fuel needed = distance × (L per 100 km ÷ 100)

Trip cost = fuel needed × price per litre. Cost per person = trip cost ÷ people sharing.

Worked examples

A 300 mile trip at 28 MPG (US) with fuel at 3.50 a gallon costs $37.50, using 40.6 L (10.71 US gal).

480 km at 7 L/100 km with fuel at 1.60 a litre costs €53.76, which is €17.92 each for three people.

Tips

  • Use the average from your car's trip computer for the closest result.
  • Highway speed, heavy loads and air conditioning all raise fuel use.
  • 1 US gallon is 3.785 litres and 1 UK gallon is 4.546 litres. Pick the right gallon.
  • Share the cost with passengers using the people field.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the fuel cost of a trip?

Divide the distance by your fuel economy to find the fuel used, then multiply by the fuel price. The calculator does both steps.

How do I convert MPG to L/100 km?

For US MPG divide 235.21 by the MPG figure. For UK MPG divide 282.48 by it. You can also just switch the unit and the number converts.

Why is UK MPG higher than US MPG?

The UK gallon is about 20% bigger, so the same car shows a higher UK MPG.

Are the default prices live?

No. They are example prices for the first load. Enter what you pay at the pump.

Estimate a trip using realistic consumption

A useful trip estimate needs the total distance, the vehicle’s typical fuel economy and the price per litre or gallon. Fuel use can change with traffic, speed, tyre pressure, load, air-conditioning, terrain and driving style. Use the same distance and volume units throughout.

Using the Fuel Cost Calculator wisely

For a round trip, include both directions and any planned detours. If the vehicle display reports litres per 100 km, do not enter that figure as kilometres per litre: those units express efficiency in opposite ways. Treat the result as a budget estimate, not a guaranteed fuel bill.
