Saudi End of Service Guide (Articles 84 and 85)

How the end-of-service award works in Saudi Arabia, step by step, with worked examples you can check against your own settlement.

What the end-of-service award is

When an employment contract in Saudi Arabia ends, the employer usually owes the worker a one-time payment called the end-of-service award (in Arabic, mukafaat nihayat al-khidma). It is separate from your last salary and from any unused leave. The rules sit in the Saudi Labor Law, mainly Article 84 for how the award is calculated and Article 85 for what you get if you resign.

Rules in this guide were last checked in October 2026. This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice.

How the award is calculated (Article 84)

The award depends on two things: your last wage and your years of service. The wage used is your last wage, which means basic salary plus the regular allowances you are paid. Part years count in proportion, so 4 years and 6 months is not rounded down.

Years of serviceAward for each year of service
First 5 yearsHalf a month of wage
Every year after the first 5One full month of wage

Example 1: employer ends the contract after 4 years

Your last wage is 8,000 SAR and you worked from 1 January 2022 to 1 January 2026. Half a month for each of 4 years is 0.5 × 8,000 × 4, so the award is SAR 16,000.00.

Example 2: 12 years of service

Your last wage is 10,000 SAR over 12 years. The first 5 years give half a month each, and the next 7 years give a full month each. The first 5 years give 0.5 × 10,000 × 5, which is 25,000 SAR, and the next 7 years give 10,000 × 7, which is 70,000 SAR. The award is SAR 95,000.00, which equals 9.5 months of wage.

If you resign (Article 85)

If the worker ends the contract by resigning, the award is reduced according to how long they served. If the employer ends the contract, or a fixed-term contract simply expires, the full award from the table above is paid.

Years of service when you resignShare of the full award you receive
Less than 2 yearsNothing
2 years up to 5 yearsOne third
More than 5 years up to 10 yearsTwo thirds
10 years or moreThe full award

Example 3: resigning after 4 years

With a last wage of 6,000 SAR and 4 years of service, the full award would be SAR 12,000.00. Because you resigned between 2 and 5 years, you receive one third, which is SAR 4,000.00.

Notice that exactly 5 years still sits in the one-third band, and the two-thirds band begins after 5 years. This is why the exact start and end dates matter, and why resigning a few days later can change the result a lot near a boundary.

Some situations give the full award even when the worker leaves, for example force majeure or the circumstances listed in Article 81. If you think you are in one of these cases, choose the full-award option in the calculator and ask the labour office to confirm.

When the award can be lost

  • Dismissal for the serious reasons listed in Article 80 can mean you lose the award.
  • There is no award for the probation period, so check your contract dates.
  • If the contract says nothing about a point, the law applies. If the contract is more generous than the law, the contract wins.

Checklist before you accept a final settlement

  1. Write down your first and last working day exactly as they appear in your contract and your records.
  2. Confirm the wage your employer used. Ask whether regular allowances such as housing and transport were included.
  3. Calculate the award yourself with the Saudi End of Service Calculator and compare.
  4. If the figures differ, ask your employer to show their calculation in writing before you sign anything.
  5. Keep your contract, payslips, bank transfers and the final settlement letter.

Common mistakes

The first mistake is using only the basic salary when the law uses your last wage including regular allowances. The second is counting one full month for every year, when the first five years are half a month each. The third is forgetting that resigning reduces the amount for service under 10 years. Finally, many people forget that the award is calculated on the wage at the end of the service, not the wage in the first year.

Frequently asked questions

Is the end-of-service award the same as the final settlement?

No. The final settlement usually includes your last salary, unused leave and other dues. The end-of-service award is one part of it.

Which wage is used to calculate the award?

Your last wage, which includes basic salary and regular allowances. Check your contract to see how your pay is defined.

Do I get the award if I resign after 3 years?

Yes, but only one third of the full award, because you resigned after 2 years and before 5 years.

Are part years counted?

Yes. Part years are counted in proportion, so months and days after full years add to the award.

Is this the official calculation?

No. It is an explanation based on Articles 84 and 85. For a dispute, contact the labour office or a labour lawyer.
