About the Currency Converter

How to use it

  1. Enter the amount and pick the currency you have.
  2. Pick the currency you want.
  3. Read the converted amount, the rate and a quick table of common amounts. Use Swap to reverse the pair.

The formula

Converted amount = amount × (rate of target currency ÷ rate of source currency)

All rates are quoted against one base currency, so any pair can be calculated.

Tips

  • Banks and transfer apps add a margin to the mid-market rate. Compare the final amount received, not just the fee.
  • Rates move during the day. For a large transfer, check again just before you send.
  • Currencies pegged to the dollar, such as the Saudi riyal and UAE dirham, change very little against the dollar.
  • Use the quick table to check typical amounts, such as monthly remittances.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the exchange rates come from?

The page loads rates from a public exchange rate service every time it opens and refreshes them regularly. Rates are updated about once a day by the provider.

Is this the rate my bank will give me?

No. This is the mid-market rate. Banks and money transfer services add a margin, so you will usually receive a little less.

What if the live rates cannot load?

The page falls back to built-in approximate rates and says so in the result. Refresh once your connection is back.

Why do SAR and AED rates barely change against the dollar?

Both are pegged to the US dollar, so their rate against the dollar stays almost fixed. Their rate against the rupee moves with the dollar rupee rate.

Why your received amount can differ

The converter uses the displayed rate source when available and may fall back to approximate built-in rates if the service cannot be reached. The rate timestamp and status in the result help you tell which situation applies. Mid-market rates are reference rates, not a guaranteed customer rate.

Using the Currency Converter wisely

Banks, card networks and remittance providers may add a spread, transfer fee or intermediary charge. Before sending money, compare the final amount the recipient will receive, the delivery time and the fee—not just the headline exchange rate. Avoid using an indicative rate as a confirmation of a transaction.
