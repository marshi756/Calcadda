About the Bahrain End of Service Calculator

Estimate the leaving indemnity in Bahrain for a private sector worker who is not covered by the Social Insurance Law, such as most expatriate employees.

Check the correct scheme and wage basis

The way end-of-service benefits are calculated may depend on the worker category, employment dates, wage definition and the applicable Bahrain scheme. Changes to contribution or benefit arrangements can mean that not every period is treated in the same way.

Using the Bahrain End of Service Calculator wisely

Confirm the applicable scheme with the relevant official authority and your employer. Keep your contract, payslips and employment dates available, and request a written calculation before accepting a final settlement.

Rates and rules in this guide were last checked in October 2026.

How to use it

  1. Enter your last monthly wage, which means your basic wage plus fixed allowances as your contract defines them.
  2. Enter your start date, your last working day and any unpaid leave days.
  3. Read the indemnity and how it splits between the first three years and later years.

The formula

First 3 years: half a month of wage for each year

After 3 years: one month of wage for each year

Part years are paid in proportion.

Worked examples

A wage of 500 BHD for 5 years: the first three years give BHD 750.000 and the next two give BHD 1,000.000. The total leaving indemnity is BHD 1,750.000.

Common mistakes to avoid

Many people count one month for every year from the start. In Bahrain the first three years are half a month each, and the full month starts from the fourth year. Also remember that unpaid leave is not counted.

Tips

  • Since March 2024, employers pay end-of-service contributions for non-Bahraini workers into the Social Insurance Organisation each month, at 4.2% of annual salary for the first three years and 8.4% after. These amounts are close to the same as the old half-month and one-month rule.
  • Unpaid leave is not counted as service.
  • Serious misconduct listed in the law can lose the indemnity, so check your case.
  • Keep your contract and payslips, which help if there is a dispute.

Frequently asked questions

How is end of service calculated in Bahrain?

Under the Labour Law for the Private Sector (Law No. 36 of 2012), a worker who is not covered by the Social Insurance Law gets half a month of wage for each of the first three years and one month of wage for each year after.

Who is covered by this calculator?

Mainly non-Bahraini workers in the private sector. Bahraini employees under the Social Insurance Law follow that system instead.

What changed in 2024?

A resolution of 2023 moved non-Bahraini end-of-service payments into monthly contributions to the Social Insurance Organisation. The monthly rates work out close to the older formula.

Is this exact for my case?

It is an estimate, last checked in October 2026. Your final amount depends on your contract and how your employer defines wages. The Labour Market Regulatory Authority can guide you.
