About the Mortgage Calculator

See what a home loan really costs each month, including property tax, home insurance and association fees, not just the principal and interest.

How to use it

  1. Enter the home price and your down payment percentage.
  2. Enter the interest rate and the loan term in years.
  3. Add property tax, insurance and fees to see the full monthly cost.

The formula

Loan = price × (1 − down payment %)

Monthly principal and interest = L × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n), then add tax, insurance and fees.

Worked examples

A 400,000 home with 20% down at 6.5% for 30 years has a loan of $320,000.00. Principal and interest are $2,022.62 a month, and with tax and insurance the payment is $2,489.28.

Tips

  • A larger down payment lowers the loan, the payment and the interest.
  • Many lenders also ask for mortgage insurance if your down payment is under 20%. Ask your lender.
  • Compare a 15 year and a 30 year loan. The shorter term has a higher payment but far less interest.
  • Do not forget closing costs, which are separate from the monthly payment.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in a mortgage payment?

Principal and interest, and often property tax and home insurance. Some homes also have association or maintenance fees.

How much should I put down?

Many buyers aim for 20% to avoid mortgage insurance, but lower down payments are common. Your lender can tell you the options.

Why is the total interest so high?

Over a long term, interest accumulates on a large balance. A shorter term or extra payments reduce it a lot.

Does this work outside the US?

Yes for the loan payment. Set property tax to 0 and leave insurance blank if they do not apply where you live.

What a mortgage payment may leave out

A principal-and-interest payment is only one part of owning a home. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, service charges, maintenance and closing costs can materially change the monthly housing budget. Some loans also have variable rates or payment resets.

Using the Mortgage Calculator wisely

Compare scenarios using the same loan amount and term, and test a higher rate if the loan is variable. Before making an offer, ask the lender for an official loan estimate and confirm local taxes and insurance separately. This calculator is not a lending decision or affordability guarantee.
