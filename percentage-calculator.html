The formula

X% of Y = X × Y ÷ 100

X is what % of Y = X ÷ Y × 100

Increase Y by X% = Y × (1 + X ÷ 100). Decrease uses (1 − X ÷ 100).

Worked examples

15% of 240 is 36.

36 is 15% of 240.

Increasing 500 by 20% gives 600.

Decreasing 1,200 by 30% gives 840.

Common mistakes to avoid

The classic error is treating "20% off then 10% off" as 30% off. The second discount applies to the reduced price. Another is dividing by the wrong number when finding what percent one value is of another: always divide by the whole you are comparing against. Check that your answer is sensible, for example that 15% of 240 is less than 240.

Tips

  • "Of" means multiply. 15% of 240 is 0.15 × 240.
  • To find 10% of a number, move the decimal one place left, then build other percentages from it.
  • An increase of 20% followed by a decrease of 20% does not return you to the start.
  • Use the percent change calculator to compare an old and a new value.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For 15% of 240, that is 15 × 240 ÷ 100 = 36.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second and multiply by 100.

How do I increase a number by a percentage?

Multiply by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. To add 20%, multiply by 1.20.

How do I find the original number?

If X is Y% of the original, the original is X ÷ (Y ÷ 100). Use the last option in the calculator.

Three common percentage questions

“What is x% of a value?” means multiply the value by x/100. “What percentage is A of B?” means A ÷ B × 100, provided B is not zero. “What is the percentage change?” compares a new value with an original baseline.

Using the Percentage Calculator wisely

For example, 15% of 200 is 30. If a bill rises from 80 to 100, the increase is 25% because the increase of 20 is divided by the original 80. Identify which question you are solving before entering numbers.
