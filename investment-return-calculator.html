About the Investment Return Calculator

Compare investments fairly. Enter what you put in and what it is worth now to see total return and the average yearly return (CAGR).

How to use it

  1. Enter the amount you invested.
  2. Enter the current or final value.
  3. Enter how many years you held it.

The formula

Total return = (Final − Invested) ÷ Invested × 100

CAGR = (Final ÷ Invested)^(1 ÷ years) − 1

Worked examples

10,000 growing to 15,000 over 4 years is a total return of 50% and an annualized return of 10.67%. At that pace money doubles in 6.8 years.

50,000 falling to 42,000 in 2 years is a loss of -$8,000.00. The annualized return is -8.35%.

Common mistakes to avoid

The classic mistake is judging an investment by total return alone. A 50% gain over ten years is far weaker than 50% over two. Always look at the annualized figure. Also remember to include fees, taxes and any extra money you added along the way, otherwise the return looks better than it really was.

Tips

  • Always compare investments by annualized return, not total return. Time changes everything.
  • Include fees and taxes in the final value for a realistic number.
  • A big loss needs an even bigger gain to recover. The calculator shows the change needed.
  • Past returns do not guarantee future returns.

Frequently asked questions

What is CAGR?

Compound annual growth rate is the steady yearly rate that would turn your starting amount into the final amount. It smooths out ups and downs.

Why is annualized return lower than total return divided by years?

Because growth compounds. CAGR accounts for earning returns on earlier returns.

Can I use this for property or a business?

Yes, for any asset where you know the amount put in, the final value and the holding period.

Does it include dividends or rent?

Only if you add them to the final value. The calculator uses just the two values you enter.

Separate contributions from investment growth

A projected return depends on the assumed rate, contribution timing and investment period. Real investments do not deliver a fixed return each year: markets can fall, returns can arrive unevenly, and fees and taxes reduce what you keep.

Using the Investment Return Calculator wisely

Try a lower-return scenario and a shorter time horizon as well as your expected case. Do not interpret the projection as a promise or use one output as a complete investment plan. Keep emergency savings separate and consider your risk tolerance before committing money.
