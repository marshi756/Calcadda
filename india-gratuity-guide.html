India Gratuity Guide: Formula, Limit and New Labour Codes

Gratuity in India explained with the formula, three worked examples and the points people most often get wrong.

What gratuity is

Gratuity is a lump sum an employer pays as a thank-you for long service when you leave a job. It is separate from your provident fund and your last salary. Most private sector employees in India are covered once they meet the service condition.

Rules in this guide were last checked in October 2026. This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice.

Who is eligible

  • Employees who complete five years of continuous service with the same employer. The exceptions are death or disability, when no minimum applies.
  • Under the new labour codes in force since 21 November 2025, fixed-term employees can qualify after one year.

The formula

For employers covered by the gratuity law:

Gratuity = last drawn (Basic + DA) × 15 ÷ 26 × years of service

The figure 26 stands for the working days in a month. For employers not covered by the law, the common method uses 30 instead of 26, and counts only completed years. In both cases the salary is Basic plus Dearness Allowance (DA), not your total CTC.

Rounding

For employers covered by the law, a period of more than six months is counted as a full year. So 10 years and 8 months is counted as 11 years, while 10 years and 5 months stays at 10.

Three worked examples

Basic + DA of ₹40,000 and 3 years of service, as a fixed-term employee who qualifies after one year: 40,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 3 is ₹69,230.77.

Basic + DA of ₹60,000 and 13 years of service: 60,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 13 is ₹4,50,000.00.

Basic + DA of ₹50,000 and 10 years 8 months. More than six months counts as a year, so 11 years are used. The gratuity is ₹3,17,307.69.

The tax-free limit

For private sector employees, gratuity is exempt from income tax up to ₹20 lakh. Anything above that is added to your income. Our calculator splits the result into the tax-free and the taxable part, and you can change the limit in the box if the rule changes.

Common mistakes

  • Using CTC instead of Basic plus DA.
  • Counting 30 days instead of 26 for employers covered by the law.
  • Ignoring the rounding rule for months beyond six.
  • Assuming everyone needs five years. Fixed-term employees may qualify after one year.

Frequently asked questions

What salary is used for gratuity?

Your last drawn Basic salary plus Dearness Allowance. HRA, bonuses and other allowances are not included.

How many years are needed?

Five years of continuous service for most employees. Fixed-term employees can qualify after one year under the new labour codes. Death and disability are exceptions.

Is gratuity taxable?

It is exempt for private sector employees up to ₹20 lakh. The excess is taxable.

What is the 15/26 formula?

It means 15 days of wages for every year of service, with a month taken as 26 working days.

Is this exact for my company?

It is an estimate. Your employer policy and how wages are defined can change the amount, so confirm with HR.
