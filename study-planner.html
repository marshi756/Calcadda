How to use a study planner

  1. Start with fixed things first: school, work, meals and sleep.
  2. Place your hardest subject in the time of day when you feel most alert.
  3. Give every study block one clear goal, such as "finish chapter 4 questions".
  4. Leave at least one free slot a day so the plan survives a bad day.

Tips for a plan you will actually follow

  • Plan no more than 3 or 4 study blocks a day to begin with.
  • Alternate subjects so you do not get tired of one.
  • Review the week every Sunday and adjust the next one.
  • Keep revision slots for old topics, not only new ones.

Frequently asked questions

How do I save the planner as a PDF?

Tap the Print button and choose Save as PDF as the destination. On a phone, use Share or Print from the browser menu.

Is what I type saved?

No. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere, so print or save the PDF before you close the page.

How many hours should I study a day?

It depends on your exam and level. Short, focused sessions of 25 to 50 minutes with breaks usually work better than long unbroken hours.

Can I change the time slots?

Yes. Tap any cell, including the time labels, and type your own text.

Build a timetable you can follow

Start by listing fixed commitments, deadlines and the subjects that need the most attention. Break a large task into sessions with a clear outcome, and add short breaks. Leave some open time for unexpected work rather than filling every available hour.

Using the Printable Study Planner wisely

Review the plan weekly and adjust it based on what was completed. A printable planner is a support tool, not a guarantee of results; sleep, spaced practice, active recall and asking for help when stuck are also important parts of effective studying.
