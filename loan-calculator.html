About the Loan Calculator

Find out what a loan really costs: the monthly payment, the total interest, and how much time and money an extra payment can save.

How to use it

  1. Enter the loan amount and the yearly interest rate your lender quoted.
  2. Enter the term in years or months.
  3. Optionally add an extra monthly payment to see how much sooner you finish.

The formula

Monthly payment = P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n)

P is the loan amount, i is the monthly rate (yearly rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments.

Worked examples

A 250,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years costs $1,580.17 a month. Over the full term you repay $568,861.22, of which $318,861.22 is interest.

A home loan of ₹25 lakh at 9% for 20 years has an EMI of ₹22,493.15 and total interest of ₹28,98,355.74.

Adding 200 a month to the first loan pays it off in 22 yr 1 mo, saving $97,618.12 in interest and 7 yr 11 mo of payments.

Tips

  • A shorter term raises the monthly payment but cuts the total interest sharply.
  • Even a small extra payment each month goes straight to the principal and saves interest for years.
  • Compare loans by total interest, not only by the monthly payment.
  • Check for processing fees and prepayment charges before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

How is a loan payment calculated?

Each payment covers that month's interest plus part of the principal. The calculator uses the standard amortization formula so every payment is equal over the whole term.

What is the difference between EMI and a monthly payment?

They mean the same thing. EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the term used in India and the Gulf, and monthly payment is used in the US and UK.

Does an extra payment really save that much?

Yes. Interest is charged on the remaining balance, so lowering the balance early reduces every later month's interest. The calculator shows the exact time and interest saved.

Does this include fees or insurance?

No. It uses only the amount, rate and term you enter. Add fees, insurance and taxes separately to see the full cost.

Compare the full cost, not just the monthly payment

A loan payment depends on principal, interest rate, term and repayment frequency. A longer term often lowers each instalment but can increase total interest. Fees, insurance, variable rates and early repayment conditions may not be included in a basic amortisation calculation.

Using the Loan Calculator wisely

Compare offers using the same principal and term, then check the total amount payable and the lender’s disclosed effective rate. Confirm whether the rate is fixed or variable and whether any introductory rate changes later. The result is an estimate, not a lender offer.
