About the FD and RD Calculator

Compare a lump-sum fixed deposit with a monthly recurring deposit. See the maturity amount, the interest earned and the effective yearly yield.

How to use it

  1. Choose Fixed deposit or Recurring deposit.
  2. Enter the amount, the interest rate and the tenure.
  3. For an FD, pick how often interest is compounded. Many banks use quarterly.

The formula

FD maturity = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × t)

RD adds each monthly deposit and compounds it for the months it stays in the account.

Worked examples

An FD of ₹1 lakh at 7% for 5 years, compounded quarterly, matures at ₹1,41,477.82. Interest earned is ₹41,477.82.

An RD of ₹5,000 a month at 6.5% for 3 years matures at ₹1,99,121.86, and you deposit ₹1,80,000.00.

Common mistakes to avoid

Comparing deposits by the headline rate alone is a trap, because quarterly compounding gives a higher effective yield than yearly. Another mistake is forgetting tax on interest, and the penalty for breaking a deposit early. For an RD, remember that each monthly deposit earns interest only for the months it stays invested.

Tips

  • Compare banks by the effective yield, not just the stated rate.
  • Senior citizens often get a higher rate. Enter that rate directly.
  • Interest on deposits is usually taxable. Check the rules where you live.
  • Breaking a deposit early usually reduces the interest. Plan the tenure carefully.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between FD and RD?

An FD is one lump sum locked for a period. An RD is a fixed amount you deposit every month for the tenure.

How often is FD interest compounded?

Many Indian banks compound quarterly. Others pay monthly or at maturity. Pick the option your bank states.

Is this the exact bank figure?

It is close, but banks may round differently or use slightly different day counts. Treat it as an estimate.

What is the effective yearly yield?

It is the real yearly return after compounding. It is higher than the stated rate when interest compounds more than once a year.

Compare deposits using the same assumptions

A fixed deposit typically locks money for a stated term, while a recurring deposit accepts regular contributions. Interest may be calculated or compounded differently by each bank and product. The displayed estimate may not include tax, premature-withdrawal penalties or special customer rates.

Using the FD and RD Calculator wisely

Before opening an account, confirm the current rate, compounding method, minimum contribution, maturity date, payout option and early-closure rules directly with the institution. Compare the net maturity amount rather than relying only on the advertised annual rate.
