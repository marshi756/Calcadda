About the Food Calorie Counter

Log what you eat and see calories and macros against your daily goal. The food list includes everyday Indian and Gulf dishes, and everything stays in your browser.

How to use it

  1. Set your daily calorie goal. Use the calorie calculator if you are not sure.
  2. Search a food, choose the meal and the number of servings, and tap it to add.
  3. Watch the bar fill up. Use plus and minus to adjust servings, or add your own food.

The formula

Calories for a food = calories per serving × number of servings

The bar shows calories eaten ÷ your daily goal.

Worked examples

One roti (104 kcal), a katori of dal (140 kcal), a katori of rice (195 kcal) and a banana (105 kcal) add up to about 544 kcal.

A chicken shawarma wrap is around 530 kcal, so with a 2,000 kcal goal it uses about a quarter of the day.

Tips

  • Log as you eat. Remembering later is how most counting goes wrong.
  • Use servings, such as 1.5 rotis, instead of guessing.
  • Add your own foods for home recipes by dividing the recipe total by the servings.
  • Do not aim for a very low goal. Talk to a doctor or dietitian first.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate are the calorie values?

They are approximate averages for common serving sizes. Home cooking, oil and portion sizes change the numbers, so treat them as a guide.

Is my food log saved?

Only in this browser on this device, for the current day. Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing site data removes it.

How do I choose a calorie goal?

Use the calorie and TDEE calculator, which estimates your needs from age, weight, height and activity.

Can I add a food that is not listed?

Yes. Open Add your own food, enter the calories (and macros if you know them) and add it.

Make food entries as comparable as possible

Food energy depends on portion size, ingredients, cooking method and added oil, sugar or sauces. Choose the closest food entry and enter the amount actually eaten. Weighing a portion or using a package label is often more consistent than estimating by eye.

Using the Food Calorie Counter wisely

Food databases and restaurant portions vary, so the total is approximate. This tool is for general tracking and is not a medical nutrition assessment. If you need a diet for diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy or another health condition, work with a qualified clinician or dietitian.
