About the Pregnancy Due Date Calculator

Estimate your baby's due date from the first day of your last period, and see how far along you are today.

How to use it

  1. Enter the first day of your last menstrual period.
  2. Enter your average cycle length. The default is 28 days.
  3. Read your estimated due date, current week, trimester and the full-term window.

The formula

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days, adjusted for your cycle length

If your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days, the date moves by the difference.

Worked examples

If your last period began on 1 September 2026, with a 28 day cycle, the estimated due date is June 8, 2027, and conception was around September 15, 2026.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using the date of the doctor visit or the test instead of the first day of the last period is a common error. Another is assuming the due date is exact. Only a small share of babies arrive on that day. If your cycles are irregular, ask your doctor about an early scan for a more reliable date.

Tips

  • Only about 4 to 5% of babies arrive on the exact due date. Most come in the two weeks around it.
  • An early ultrasound can give a more precise date if your cycles are irregular.
  • Full term is 37 to 42 weeks.
  • Use the date to plan checkups, and always follow your doctor's advice.

Frequently asked questions

How is the due date calculated?

By adding 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last period, using Naegele's rule, and adjusting if your cycle is not 28 days.

How accurate is the estimate?

It is a guide. Ultrasound measurements in early pregnancy can be more accurate, and your doctor may adjust the date.

What if my cycles are irregular?

Enter your usual cycle length, but the date may be less reliable. Ask your doctor about an early scan.

Which trimester am I in?

The first trimester lasts to the end of week 13, the second from week 14 to 27 and the third from week 28 onward.

Due dates are estimates

A due-date calculation commonly estimates 40 weeks from the first day of the last menstrual period, assuming a typical cycle. Ovulation timing, cycle length and the information available can shift the estimate. The date alone cannot confirm gestational age or pregnancy health.

Using the Pregnancy Due Date Calculator wisely

A healthcare professional may adjust dating using menstrual history or ultrasound findings. Contact your maternity-care provider for personalised guidance, especially if dates are uncertain or you have pain, bleeding or other concerning symptoms. Do not use a calculator result to delay urgent care.
