About the SIP Calculator

A systematic investment plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount every month. See what it could grow to, with an optional yearly step-up and an inflation-adjusted view.

How to use it

  1. Enter your monthly SIP amount and the return you expect per year.
  2. Enter the number of years.
  3. Optionally add a yearly step-up and expected inflation.

The formula

Each month: balance = (balance + SIP) × (1 + monthly return)

With step-up, the SIP amount rises by your chosen percentage every 12 months.

Worked examples

A SIP of ₹5,000 a month at 12% for 10 years invests ₹6,00,000.00 and grows to about ₹11,61,695.38. In today's money that is roughly ₹6,48,684.63.

If you raise the SIP by 10% every year, the same plan reaches about ₹16,87,163.13.

Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is stopping a SIP when markets fall, which locks in losses and misses the recovery. Others pick a return that is too high. Treat the result as one scenario, try a lower rate as well, and check the value in today's money so inflation does not surprise you.

Tips

  • Increasing your SIP each year with your salary can grow the final amount a lot.
  • Use a realistic return. Equity returns are never guaranteed and vary year to year.
  • Stay invested through market falls. SIPs work through long periods.
  • Check the value in today's money. Inflation quietly reduces what the future amount can buy.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SIP?

A SIP is a way to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month, rather than all at once. It spreads your buying across different market prices.

What return should I assume?

Many planners use 10 to 12% for long-term equity funds as a rough guide, and less for debt funds. These are assumptions, not promises.

What is a step-up SIP?

It increases your monthly amount by a set percentage every year, usually in line with salary growth.

Does this show actual fund returns?

No. It is a projection based on the rate you enter. Actual returns are not fixed and can be negative.

Understand SIP projections

A systematic investment plan (SIP) calculator estimates how regular contributions may grow at an assumed rate. Mutual-fund returns fluctuate, and the order of market gains and losses matters. Fees, taxes, missed contributions and contribution timing can all affect the actual result.

Using the SIP Calculator wisely

Compare several return assumptions instead of relying on one optimistic number. The projection is not a guaranteed return, a forecast of market performance or personal investment advice. Consider your goals, time horizon and ability to tolerate losses before investing.
