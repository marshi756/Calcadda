About the Calorie and TDEE Calculator

Find out how many calories your body burns each day and what to eat to lose, maintain or gain weight, with a protein, carb and fat split.

How to use it

  1. Enter your sex, age, weight and height.
  2. Choose your activity level and your goal.
  3. Read your daily calories and macros. Then log your food in the calorie counter.

The formula

BMR (men) = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5. For women the last term is −161.

TDEE = BMR × activity factor. Goal calories = TDEE adjusted by about 1,100 kcal a day per kg of weekly change.

Worked examples

A 30 year old woman, 65 kg and 160 cm, lightly active: BMR 1,339 kcal, maintenance 1,841 kcal. To lose 0.5 kg a week she would eat about 1,291 kcal.

Tips

  • A steady loss of 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week is easier to keep than a crash diet.
  • The calculator never goes below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women. Go lower only with medical supervision.
  • Protein helps you feel full and protects muscle while losing weight.
  • Recalculate every few kilos, because your needs change as your weight changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure is the calories you burn in a day, including exercise and daily movement. Eating at that level keeps your weight steady.

How accurate is this?

It is a good estimate for most adults, usually within about 10%. Real needs vary, so adjust based on how your weight changes over two to three weeks.

Is it safe to eat very few calories?

Very low intakes can cause muscle loss and nutrient shortages. If you have a history of disordered eating, speak to a doctor or dietitian before dieting.

Why is it only for adults?

The equations are designed for adults. Growing teens need different guidance from a doctor.

What the calorie estimate means

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) estimates energy used at rest. Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) adjusts that estimate for the activity level selected. The result is an estimate, not a measurement: actual needs vary with body composition, health, medication, sleep and day-to-day activity.

Using the Calorie and TDEE Calculator wisely

Use the result as a starting point and compare it with changes in energy, hunger and weight over several weeks. Activity multipliers can overestimate or underestimate real movement. People who are pregnant, under 18, managing a medical condition or recovering from an eating disorder should seek personalised advice from a qualified clinician or dietitian.
