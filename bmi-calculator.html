About the BMI Calculator

Check your body mass index in metric or US units, see your healthy weight range, and switch to Asian cut-offs, which use lower thresholds.

How to use it

  1. Pick metric (kg, cm) or US units (lb, feet, inches).
  2. Enter your weight and height.
  3. Tick Asian cut-offs if that applies to you, then read your BMI, category and healthy range.

The formula

BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in meters)²

Standard cut-offs: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 healthy, 25 to 29.9 overweight, 30 and above obese.

Worked examples

70 kg at 170 cm gives a BMI of 24.2, which is in the Healthy weight range. The healthy range for that height is 53.5 kg to 72 kg.

68 kg at 165 cm with Asian cut-offs gives a BMI of 25 and falls in the Overweight category.

Tips

  • BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Muscle, age and body shape are not considered.
  • People of South Asian and some other Asian origins face health risks at lower BMI, so lower cut-offs of 23 and 25 are often used.
  • Waist size is another useful measure. Talk to a doctor about your results.
  • This calculator is for adults. Children need age-based charts.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy. With Asian cut-offs the healthy range ends at 22.9.

Why use Asian cut-offs?

Studies show that people of Asian descent can have higher body fat and health risk at a lower BMI than other groups, so lower thresholds are recommended.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so a muscular person can have a high BMI without excess fat.

Can I use this during pregnancy?

No. BMI does not apply in the same way during pregnancy. Ask your doctor or midwife.

How BMI is calculated

BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. For example, 70 kg at 1.75 m gives 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75), or about 22.9. Check that height is entered in the requested unit and that weight is current.

Using the BMI Calculator wisely

BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis. It does not directly measure body fat or distinguish muscle from fat. Interpretation can differ for children, pregnancy, older adults and some ethnic groups. Discuss concerns with a qualified health professional rather than making a health decision from BMI alone.
