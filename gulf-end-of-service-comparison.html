End of Service in the Gulf: Country Comparison

The six Gulf end-of-service systems side by side, with a link to the calculator for each country.

End of service benefits in the Gulf at a glance

If you work in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman or Bahrain, your employer usually owes you an end-of-service payment when the job ends. The rules look similar, but the details change from country to country. This guide puts them side by side in one table, so you can see how your years of service count before you use the calculator for your country.

Rules in this guide were last checked in October 2026.

How to check your own case

  1. Find your last wage and check whether your country uses basic wage or total wage.
  2. Count your service from the first day to your last working day, and remove unpaid leave if your country excludes it.
  3. Open the calculator for your country below and enter your wage and dates.
  4. Compare the result with your final settlement letter. If the numbers differ, ask your employer to show the calculation.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is using the total salary where the law uses the basic wage, or the other way round. Another is counting the same rate for every year, when most countries pay half a month in the early years and a full month later. Some people also forget that resignation can reduce the amount in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait. Finally, remember that your contract can promise more than the legal minimum.

Frequently asked questions

Which Gulf country pays the most end of service?

It depends on your years of service and wage, not only on the country. Rules differ on the rate for the first years, on caps and on resignation. Use the country calculators with your own numbers to compare.

Do I get end of service if I resign?

Often yes, but in some countries the amount is reduced if you resign early in your service. Saudi Arabia and Kuwait use tiers by years of service, so check the table above and your contract.

Which salary is used for the calculation?

It differs. The UAE, Qatar and Oman use the basic wage. Saudi Arabia uses the last wage including regular allowances. Kuwait uses the last remuneration, and Bahrain follows the wage defined in your contract.

Are these figures official?

No. They are a plain-language summary and estimate. Laws and ministry guidance change, and your contract may offer more. Confirm with the labour ministry of your country or a qualified lawyer.

How to compare country estimates fairly

Do not compare headline benefit amounts without checking the assumptions behind them. Countries may define eligible wages differently, use different service-period rules, and treat resignation, dismissal, fixed-term contracts or special employment categories differently. A formula that fits one country should not be copied to another.

Before using a comparison for a job decision

Confirm the current official rule for the country and employment arrangement, then check the wage basis, start and end dates, unpaid leave, and reason for leaving. Ask HR for a written breakdown and keep your contract and payslips. This guide is for initial orientation; it is not legal advice or a guarantee of the amount payable.
