Oman and Bahrain End of Service Guide

Oman splits your service into an old-law part and a new-law part. Bahrain pays half a month, then a full month. Here is how both work.

Oman: two parts, two rules

Oman issued a new Labour Law (Royal Decree 53/2023) in 2023. For gratuity, the Ministry of Labour has explained that service is calculated in two parts for workers who are not covered by the Social Protection Law, mainly expatriate workers.

Rules in this guide were last checked in October 2026. This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice.

Period of serviceGratuity
Before the new law (up to 31 July 2023)15 days of basic wage for each of the first 3 years, then one month for each later year
After the new lawAt least one full month of basic wage for each year

The base is your last basic wage. Part years are paid in proportion. This guide uses 31 July 2023 as the date that separates the two parts. Confirm the exact date for your case with the Ministry of Labour, because sources describe the effective date slightly differently.

Oman example 1: service on both sides of the new law

Basic wage of 400 OMR, working from 1 January 2020 to 1 January 2026. Before the new law you served 3.58 years, which gives OMR 832.877. After it you served 2.42 years, which gives OMR 967.763. The total is OMR 1,800.639.

Oman example 2: service only under the new law

Basic wage of 500 OMR for 2 years from 1 January 2024. One month for each year gives OMR 1,000.000.

Bahrain: half a month, then a full month

Under the Labour Law for the Private Sector (Law No. 36 of 2012), a worker who is not covered by the Social Insurance Law receives half a month of wage for each of the first three years and one month of wage for each year after that. Part years are paid in proportion. The wage follows the definition in your contract, usually basic plus fixed allowances.

Years of serviceIndemnity for each year
First 3 yearsHalf a month of wage
Years after the first 3One month of wage

Bahrain example

A wage of 500 BHD over 5 years gives BHD 750.000 for the first three years and BHD 1,000.000 for the next two. The total leaving indemnity is BHD 1,750.000.

What changed in 2024

Since March 2024, employers pay end-of-service contributions for non-Bahraini workers into the Social Insurance Organisation every month. The rates are 4.2% of annual salary for the first three years and 8.4% after. These amounts are close to the same as the older half-month and one-month rule. Ask your employer or the Social Insurance Organisation how your own account is being credited.

Common mistakes

  • In Oman, using one formula for all years instead of splitting service at the new-law date.
  • In Oman, using total salary instead of basic wage.
  • In Bahrain, giving a full month from the first year.
  • In Bahrain, forgetting that the monthly social insurance contributions now cover part of this.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Oman use two formulas?

Because the new Labour Law of 2023 changed the rate. Service before the new law uses the older rule, and service after it uses the new rule.

Which wage is used in Oman?

The last basic wage, as confirmed by the Ministry of Labour.

Who gets the Bahrain indemnity?

Mainly non-Bahraini private sector workers who are not covered by the Social Insurance Law.

What are the Bahrain monthly contributions?

Since 2024 employers pay 4.2% of annual salary for the first three years of service and 8.4% after that into the Social Insurance Organisation for non-Bahraini workers.

Are these figures official?

No. They are estimates based on public guidance, last checked in October 2026. Confirm with the labour ministry or authority.
