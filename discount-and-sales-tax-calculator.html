About the Discount and Sales Tax Calculator

See what you will really pay. Apply one or two discounts, then add sales tax, or treat the tax as already included like GST and VAT.

How to use it

  1. Enter the original price and the discount percentage. Add a second discount if there is one.
  2. Enter the tax rate and choose whether tax is added on top or already included.
  3. Read the final price, how much you save and the tax amount.

The formula

Price after discount = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100)

With added tax: final price = price after discount × (1 + tax ÷ 100)

Worked examples

A 120 item with 25% off and 8.25% sales tax: the price after discount is $90.00, tax is $7.43, and you pay $97.43.

A ₹5,000 item at 20% off with 18% GST already included costs ₹4,000.00, and ₹610.17 of that is tax.

Common mistakes to avoid

Adding discounts together is the usual error. Two discounts multiply, so 20% and 10% give 28%, not 30%. Another is subtracting tax from a tax-inclusive price by taking a percentage off. Choose the Included option instead so the calculator divides correctly.

Tips

  • Two discounts of 20% and 10% do not make 30%. They multiply to 28%.
  • In many countries the tax is already in the shelf price. Choose Included for those.
  • Check whether a coupon applies before or after tax in the store.
  • Compare the final price per unit, not just the percentage off.

Frequently asked questions

How do stacked discounts work?

The second discount applies to the already-reduced price. The effective discount row shows the combined result.

Is sales tax added before or after the discount?

Usually after. Tax is charged on the discounted price in most places.

What if tax is already included?

Choose Already included. The calculator keeps the final price the same and shows how much of it is tax.

Can I use it for a markup?

Yes. Enter 0 for discount and use the tax field as a markup percentage.

Apply discounts and tax in the right order

A discount is normally applied to the original selling price before sales tax is calculated on the discounted taxable amount, but actual invoice rules depend on the jurisdiction and transaction. A displayed percentage is only as reliable as the rate and order you select.

Using the Discount and Sales Tax Calculator wisely

Example: a 20% discount on 100 reduces the price to 80. If a 10% tax then applies to 80, tax is 8 and the total is 88. This is an arithmetic example, not a statement of the rules for every location. Check whether a quoted price already includes tax.
