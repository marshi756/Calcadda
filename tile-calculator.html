About the Tile Calculator

Find out how many tiles a floor needs. Enter the room size, the size of one tile and a waste allowance, and get the number of tiles to buy.

What this calculator estimates

This tool estimates the number of floor tiles for a room. It finds the floor area, adds a waste allowance for cuts and breakage, and divides by the area of one tile. The result is rounded up to a whole number of tiles, because you cannot buy part of a tile.

Formula

Floor area = length × width (in metres). Area of one tile = tile length × tile width (converted from cm to metres). Tiles needed = floor area × (1 + waste %) ÷ area of one tile. Round up to the next whole tile.

Worked example

A room is 4 m by 3 m, so the floor is 12 m². You plan 60 cm by 60 cm tiles, which are 0.36 m² each. With 10% waste, the area to cover is 12 × 1.1 = 13.2 m². Divide by 0.36 to get 36.7 tiles. Round up and buy 37 tiles.

Step-by-step instructions

1. Measure the room length and width in metres. 2. Enter the tile length and width in centimetres. 3. Enter your waste allowance. A common choice is 5% for simple rooms, and 10% or more for diagonal layouts. 4. Read the number of tiles to buy.

Tips for a realistic estimate

Diagonal layouts, patterns and many cuts need more waste than a straight layout. Check the box label for the number of tiles per box, because you may need to round up to full boxes. Buy extra tiles from the same batch for future repairs, since colours can vary between batches.

Choosing the right tile size

Larger tiles mean fewer joints and a cleaner look in big rooms, but they are harder to lay on uneven floors and may need a thicker adhesive. Small tiles suit bathrooms and curved areas. Whatever size you pick, make sure the tile is rated for floor use, and that the box shows the coverage per box so you can compare it with the calculator result.

Frequently asked questions

Why add waste? Some tiles break, and some must be cut to fit walls and corners. How much should I add? Five percent is common for straight layouts. Ten percent or more suits diagonal layouts. Does this include grout? No, grout is a separate purchase. Estimate it from the tile size, joint width and tile thickness given by the grout maker.

Limits of this estimate

This tool covers flat rectangular floors. It does not handle L-shaped rooms, stairs or irregular walls, unless you split them into rectangles and add the results.
