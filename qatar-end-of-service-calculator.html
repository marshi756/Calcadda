About the Qatar End of Service Calculator

Estimate the minimum end-of-service gratuity in Qatar from your last basic wage and your service dates.

Review the contract and service period

The final end-of-service amount can depend on eligible service, the wage basis, contract terms and the employment rules that apply to the individual. A calculator uses simplified assumptions and may not account for every exception or dispute.

Using the Qatar End of Service Calculator wisely

Confirm your employment dates and the wage components included in the calculation. For a final settlement, compare the estimate with current official guidance and request a written breakdown from HR. Seek qualified advice if the service period or entitlement is disputed.

Rates and rules in this guide were last checked in October 2026.

How to use it

  1. Enter your last basic monthly wage.
  2. Enter your start date and last working day.
  3. Read the minimum gratuity and the days of pay counted.

The formula

Gratuity = basic wage ÷ 30 × 21 days × years of service

At least one year of service is required, and part years count in proportion.

Worked examples

A basic wage of 7,000 for 4 years: 7,000 ÷ 30 × 21 × 4 = QAR 19,600.00, which is 84 days (3 weeks per year).

Tips

  • Qatar law sets a minimum. Your contract or company policy may give more, so check both.
  • Basic wage is used. Allowances are not counted unless your contract says so.
  • Keep your contract, payslips and the end-of-service letter for your records.
  • If your employer disputes the amount, you can raise it with the Ministry of Labour.

Frequently asked questions

What does Qatar law say about gratuity?

Article 54 of the Labour Law gives at least three weeks of basic wage for each year of service, after at least one year.

Can my contract give more than the law?

Yes. The law sets the minimum, and a contract may offer a better amount.

Is there a reduction if I resign?

The minimum rule in Article 54 does not reduce the amount for resignation, but check your contract and the current Ministry guidance for your case.

Is this exact for my case?

It is an estimate of the legal minimum, last checked in October 2026. Your actual entitlement depends on your contract.
